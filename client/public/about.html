About SIM XR

The training layer for Physical AI.

SIM XR builds the data infrastructure for humanoid robotics. Robotics teams need millions of teleoperation demonstrations to train manipulation policies — data today is captured by in-house teams in lab studios at roughly $50K–$100K per fully-loaded operator per year. It does not scale to the millions-of-demos requirement modern Vision-Language-Action models need.

Our bet: the workforce that records this data is already deployed. Tens of millions of Quest 3, Vision Pro, and Pico 4 Ultra headsets sit idle in living rooms. An operator at home wears their own headset and teleoperates a simulated humanoid robot inside a photoreal 3D-Gaussian-Splat reconstruction of the customer's real environment. The hand-motion data is real. The action stream is real. The scene is rendered. The robot is simulated. We ship customer-shaped demonstration datasets in days, not months.

Business model

How SIM XR makes money.

Per-episode pricing on customer-shaped demonstration datasets. A target customer is a humanoid robotics team training manipulation policies — they pay for completed, quality-validated episodes recorded against their robot and their environment. The two structural advantages we sell against in-house teleop programs are cost per episode (consumer hardware + at-home operators) and scenario coverage (one captured environment multiplied into many randomized training scenes via object, task, and layout variation).

Who pays

Humanoid robotics teams training manipulation policies

VLA fine-tuners, behavior-cloning teams, simulation-to-real research groups.

What we sell

Demonstration episodes, customer-shaped

Recorded in the customer's 3DGS-reconstructed environment with their robot's embodiment and the operator network's hand-motion data.

How it scales

Consumer VR as supply, sim multiplication as leverage

Operators connect from existing consumer headsets. One captured scene yields many randomized training variants.
