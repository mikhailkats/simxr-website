Why it stalls

Why the next job is never free.

01

Every new job is a new skill.

Your model generalises across a category, not across a workplace. Every deployment adds steps nobody trained for, and every one of them needs demonstrations. Collecting those demonstrations costs robot-hours you would rather spend shipping.

02

The world the robot must learn in does not exist yet.

Training in simulation needs the customer’s room, their objects, their clutter and their lighting. Scans come back noisy, meshes are not physics-ready, and rebuilding all of it by hand is nobody’s favourite sprint.

03

The policy fails and nobody can say why.

A deployed policy misses and the default answer is “collect more data”. Sometimes that is right. Often the real cause is a missing input, a training recipe or the inference loop — and more data buys nothing at all.

01

Environment

The world the job lives in, in simulation.

  • Your robot made drivable and physics-ready
  • The scene — from your assets, CAD, or a phone walkthrough
  • Objects rebuilt from photographs: collision, mass, grasp
  • Task and success criteria, agreed and written in code
  • One scene or several; one task per scene or many
  • Parallel copies with instant reset
02

Demonstrations

Human skill, recorded in the robot’s own action space.

  • Retargeting, clamps and grip tuned to your embodiment
  • A server fleet sized to the deadline, close to the operators
  • Our operators — onboarded, qualified, working in parallel
  • Live quality control: signal freshness, episode slicing, accept / reject
  • Start-state spread designed for training, not left to chance
  • Champion selection: the few demonstrations worth multiplying
  • Delivered in your data contract — the same files your validator accepts
03

Augmentation

Hundreds of demonstrations become thousands of episodes.

  • Only the strongest demonstrations seed the generator, annotated into subtasks
  • Generation parameters set per task — the failure modes are catalogued
  • Parallel generation on a fleet, with seeds and shards under control
  • Every episode replay-validated; rejects are our cost
  • Export in LeRobot format or your own schema, validator-clean
04

Post-training

The model you already run, taught the job.

  • Conversion to the model’s data contract
  • Recipe from measured runs: batch, steps, cameras, horizon
  • Model-agnostic: the policy family you already use, or your own
  • Compute sized for cost and speed; checkpoints at every milestone
  • The best checkpoint chosen on the evaluation protocol, not on training loss
  • Reproducible: environment manifest and a rerun that brings the whole run back
05

Evaluation

One protocol. Honest numbers.

  • Fixed layouts, paired runs, seeds shared with you
  • Sample sizes matched to the claim, with confidence intervals
  • Failures decomposed by stage of the task, with video
  • Levers ranked by cost: horizon, chunking, start pose, cameras
  • The decision: which stage the loop re-enters

Case 02 · Full project

Four tasks in two scenes for a humanoid manufacturer.

0 → 65%success across all four tasks, customer’s criterion
~1,500successful demonstrations from ~20 operator-hours
~10,000generated episodes, every batch accepted

The customer brought their own robot, two scenes and a strict data contract. We made the robot drivable in VR, rebuilt the scenes, recorded the demonstrations across four tasks with our operators, multiplied them into validated episodes and delivered every batch in the customer’s own format — each one accepted by their validator. Post-trained on that data, a policy that scored zero on every task reached 65 % success across all four under the customer’s own criterion, in simulation — and 80–85 % once the acceptance-side defects we reported are corrected.
